# Chapter Design

Read this before designing or building any chapter pattern. Part 1 is the
framework for design decisions. Part 2 is the structural reference for
building patterns.

---

## Part 1 — Design Principles and Systems

### The core principle

The chapter design is not a container for the story. It is the story made
visual, audible, and felt. The reader should feel as though they have entered
the chapter's world — not that they are reading about it from outside.

Every design decision answers the question: *what is it like to be in this
moment?* Layout, colour, movement, and sound are not decorative layers
applied to the prose. They are the prose's other registers. A chapter about
cold should feel cold. A chapter about waiting should feel like waiting. A
chapter in which the wilderness is watching should feel watched.

The wilderness itself is a design presence. It is ancient, inhabited, and
continuous — it has memory, intention, and a relationship with him that
predates his understanding of it. When the wilderness is the subject of a
chapter, the design serves the place, not only his experience of it.

Some design works cumulatively. A motif introduced in one chapter may not
resolve until thirty chapters later. The reader who has been paying attention
should feel that resolution physically — in the sketch, the colour, the
sound, the layout — before they understand it intellectually.

No chapter should feel like any other chapter. The design system provides a
foundation — it does not provide a formula. Every chapter deserves genuine
creative consideration before reaching for a familiar pattern.

Reduced motion is always respected — the default for all movement is
stillness. Sound is always the reader's choice — the default is silence.

---

### The four sources

Every chapter draws from one or more of these sources. The best chapters draw
from all four simultaneously.

**1. The content**
What is happening in this specific chapter. What he is doing, thinking,
feeling. The content is always the primary source. The design should never be
more interesting than what it contains.

**2. The world**
The physical environment at the time of writing. Season, weather, time of
day, location. A midwinter chapter feels different from a high summer chapter
not because of what he writes but because of where and when he writes it.

**3. The arc**
Whose story is foregrounded. Each arc has a register:

| Arc | Register |
|---|---|
| The Boy | Practical, observational, wry, gradually more honest |
| Elara | Warmer, more careful, slightly uncertain, increasingly open |
| Margaret | Tender, then quietly grieving |
| Daniel | Recognition, discomfort, generosity |
| Spine | Weighted, returning, the buried thing always present beneath |
| The Wilderness | Ancient, indifferent, vast; design serves the place, not only his experience |
| Milestone | Meta, brief, wry about itself |

**4. The one-off**
Some chapters resist the vocabulary entirely. The design system should never
become a formula. A one-off is not a reward for importance — it is a
recognition that this particular chapter cannot be served by any existing
combination of sources, and that something entirely its own is the only
honest response.

---

### The movement vocabulary

Movement should always feel like it belongs to the natural world he lives in.
It should never call attention to itself. If the reader notices the effect
before they notice the story, the effect is wrong.

**Environmental effects**
The physical world behaving as it does. Rain falls. Snow drifts. Fire
breathes. Water moves. Lightning breaks the dark. These effects are grounded
in the chapter's literal conditions. They are not metaphor. It was raining.
It was raining.

**Atmospheric presence**
Very slow, very subtle shifts in the background — pulse, shimmer, drift.
These operate below the threshold of conscious notice. Two sources feed them:

- *Seasonal* — the warmth of late summer, the cold stillness of deep winter,
  the thaw of first spring
- *The wilderness* — older and slower than season; the sense that the place
  is aware, that something ancient is present beneath the surface of the
  chapter

These two registers can coexist but should not be confused. Seasonal
atmosphere belongs to the weather. Wilderness atmosphere belongs to the place.

**Other effects**
Any effect not covered above may serve a specific chapter if it earns its
place. The test: does this effect tell the reader something about this moment
that no other design element can? If yes, it belongs. If not, it doesn't.

---

### The layout vocabulary

Layout is one of the primary ways a chapter communicates what it is before
the reader reads a word. These are starting points, not constraints. A
chapter that needs something not listed here should have it.

**Field note** — the default. A constrained content column with generous
white space. The writing is small on the page the way field notes are small
in the world. The minimal variant takes this further — very little on the
page, the space itself carrying meaning.

**Full bleed** — the background fills the viewport. Content exists inside the
scene rather than above it. The reader is placed inside the chapter's
physical world.

**Asymmetric split** — the page divided into two unequal columns held in
tension. Text against image, presence against absence, two things that cannot
occupy the same space.

**Document** — the page becomes a primary document: a map, a botanical page,
a technical drawing. Prose becomes annotation rather than primary content.

**Shifting column** — the text column moves as the chapter progresses,
reflecting a change in voice, state, or situation. The instability of the
layout is the meaning.

---

### The standard for creative ambition

Every chapter is an entry point into a specific moment in thirty years of a
life. The reader has stepped out of whatever they were doing and into this
man's world. The design should honour that.

Before reaching for a familiar layout, ask: what is the most interesting,
most honest way to present this specific chapter? Not interesting as a design
exercise — interesting as an answer to the question *what is it like to be
here, now, in this moment?*

The layout vocabulary is a starting point. It documents what has been done.
It does not limit what can be done. A chapter told entirely through a
hand-drawn diagram. A chapter where the text bleeds into the background image
at the edges. A chapter where the typography itself fractures to reflect
fractured thinking. A chapter with no text visible until the reader scrolls
past an image. These are not hypotheticals — they are the kinds of decisions
that make a chapter feel like it could only ever be this chapter.

Bland is the only failure. A chapter that could be any chapter has failed its
moment. The design system exists to prevent repetition, not to enable it. Use
it as a foundation and then ask what this specific chapter needs that nothing
else in the story has needed before.

---

### The typographic register

**Scale signals emotional state:**

| Scale | Register | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `7-xl` / `6-xl` | Declaration | Title moments, major revelations |
| `5-xl` / `4-xl` | Chapter title | Post title block, standard |
| `3-xl` / `2-xl` | Heading | Section breaks, pull quotes |
| `xl` / `lg` | Elevated body | Opening lines, emotional peaks |
| `md` | Body | Standard field note prose |
| `sm` | Secondary | Observation logs, annotations, captions |
| `xs` / `2-xs` | Margin | Smallest annotations, dateline, location stamp |

**Font family as register:**

- **Playfair Display** — the public voice at its most formal and deliberate.
  Titles, declarations, emotional peaks. What he says when he has chosen his
  words carefully.
- **Lora** — the field note voice. Practical, observational, honest. The
  default register for most of what he writes.
- **Caveat** — the private hand. Datelines, captions, annotations,
  navigation, the buried chapters. The voice that was writing before anyone
  was reading. When Caveat dominates a chapter, the reader is closer to him
  than he intended.

**Degrees of commitment** — not everything he writes carries equal weight:

- *Opacity* — text at reduced opacity sits at a remove; he is less certain,
  less willing, or writing from a secondary register
- *Strikethrough* — he crossed it out but left it legible. The crossing-out
  is deliberate. The legibility is too.
- *The dash* — a sentence that starts and stops. What follows was written
  elsewhere, or not at all.

**Prose weight and audio weight** — in most chapters prose carries the full
weight. In played chapters audio carries some or all of it — prose becomes
minimal, a frame for the sound. In the most extreme played chapters there is
no prose at all. The relationship between prose weight and audio weight is
itself a design decision and the two must always be considered together.

---

### The sound vocabulary

Sound is a full design dimension. Every chapter has a sound world — the
toggle offers the reader entry into it. The default is silence.

**Silence** — wilderness silence is not the absence of sound. It has texture,
weight, and season. A deep winter silence is different from a late summer
silence. Designing silence means knowing what the world sounds like when
nothing is happening.

**Ambient sound** — the world sounding like itself. Fire breathing, water
moving, wind in the canopy, the quality of air before snow. Ambient sound
loops. It should be noticed on arrival and then forgotten.

**The played chapters** — his instrument carries some or all of the chapter's
weight. Prose is minimal or absent. The sound is the content. The instrument
changes across the story — a reader who listens to every played chapter will
hear it become something unrecognisable from what it was at the start.

**Designing sound with Suno** — each chapter's sound is generated via Suno.
A good prompt carries:

- The physical environment — season, weather, time of day, location
- The emotional register — the arc, the chapter's feeling, what the reader
  should sense beneath the prose
- The format — sound effect or composition, looping or single play,
  approximate duration
- Any specific sonic details — the quality of the silence, the particular
  instrument, the presence or absence of melody

The Suno prompt is part of the design brief. Write it with the same care as
the layout or variation choice.

### The variation system

Each chapter is assigned one primary style variation. The variation controls
the full colour world of the chapter — parchment tones, ink tones,
handwritten tones, rule and separator colours.

**File naming** — variation files use a two-part prefix:

```
section-{type}-{slug}
```

WordPress applies this as a class prefixed with `is-style-`:
`is-style-section-season-late-summer`, `is-style-section-arc-spine`, etc.

**Season variations** — the colour world of the chapter's physical
environment. Four foundation points:

| Slug | Character |
|---|---|
| `section-season-late-summer` | Warm parchment, sepia ink, amber accents. The default world. |
| `section-season-early-autumn` | Deeper, cooler parchment, warmth carried by the ink rather than the page. |
| `section-season-deep-winter` | Near-black, cool grey ink, minimal. The world reduced to its essentials. |
| `section-season-midwinter` | Slightly lighter than deep winter, the same cold register. |

New season variations are built as chapters require them, using these four as
reference points for tone and distance.

**Arc variations** — foregrounded arc changes the colour world. Arc
variations inflect the season base or, when the arc is strong enough,
override it entirely:

| Slug | Character |
|---|---|
| `section-arc-spine` | Warm parchment with weight and gravity. The buried thing present beneath the surface. |
| `section-arc-wilderness` | Ancient, cool, vast. Older than any season. The colour world of the place itself. |

**State variations** — the condition of writing overrides season and arc.
Applied when the mode of the chapter is the primary design fact:

| Slug | Character |
|---|---|
| `section-state-buried` | The private notebook. Caveat dominant, no ruled lines. Written for no one. |
| `section-state-survival` | Crisis. The darkest register. Everything reduced to what matters. |
| `section-state-storm` | Environment so extreme it becomes its own colour world. |

**One-off variations** — created when no existing variation serves the
chapter honestly. Named for the chapter (`section-chapter-{slug}`). Not
reused.

**Coupled variations** — when season and arc must speak simultaneously, a
coupled variation is created rather than forcing a choice. Named for both,
arc first: `section-arc-spine-season-midwinter`. Built as the story requires
them, not anticipated in advance.

**Precedence** — when season and arc are both present, the variation that
best serves the chapter takes precedence. The question is always: which
colour world does the reader need to be inside for this specific moment?

## Part 2 — Pattern Structure and Recurring Elements

### The anatomy of a chapter pattern

Every chapter pattern is a PHP file under `patterns/chapter/`. File naming:

- Numbered chapters: `chapter-001-the-clearing.php`
- Buried chapters: `chapter-001b-the-same-night.php`

Each pattern is a complete, standalone document — the full chapter experience
from navigation in to navigation out. The pattern is built from nested layers,
each with a canonical name:

**Entry** — the outermost Group block. Full-width, constrained layout, with
padding on all sides. Carries the variation class
(`is-style-section-{type}-{slug}`) that controls the chapter's full colour
world. May carry a background image. Everything else lives inside it.

**Frame** — a `<main>` Group block inside Entry. Full-width, constrained
layout. Separates the chapter content from the Waypoint above and Story
Navigation below.

**Chapter** — an `<article>` Group block inside Frame. Full-width,
constrained layout. Contains the Chapter Header, Chapter Content, and Chapter
Footer.

**Chapter Header** — a `<header>` Group block inside Chapter. Contains the
dateline and post title by default, followed by a separator. Both the
contents and the separator are variable — the header should serve the
chapter's opening moment, not a fixed template.

**Chapter Content** — a Group block inside Chapter with the
`is-style-container-prose` class. Contains everything the reader reads:
prose, sketches, logs, diagrams, audio, effects. This is where each
chapter's unique design lives.

**Chapter Footer** — a `<footer>` Group block inside Chapter with the
`is-style-container-meta` class. Contains the chapter's closing stamp — by
default the bird horizon icon, post title, inline separators, day fragment,
and season fragment. Variable per chapter.

---

### Fixed elements

These elements appear in every chapter pattern without exception. Their inner
content is locked and never changes — only their wrapper may vary.

**Waypoint Content (Chapter)**
Pattern: `x3p0-a-boy-in-the-wild/waypoint-content-chapter`

The persistent site header at the top of every chapter. Contains the site
title, an inline separator, the chapter's Era taxonomy term, and two toggles
— the audio toggle and the day/night toggle.

**Story Navigation Content**
Pattern: `x3p0-a-boy-in-the-wild/story-navigation-content`

Previous and next chapter navigation at the bottom of every chapter.
Previous on the left, next on the right, both showing the linked chapter's
title.

---

### Default elements

These elements are present in every chapter pattern by default. They may be
changed when the chapter has a reason to change them. No chapter should feel
constrained by these defaults.

**Waypoint wrapper**
Pattern: `x3p0-a-boy-in-the-wild/waypoint-default-full`

Full-width wrapper for the Waypoint Content. A chapter may use a different
wrapper if the layout calls for it, as long as it still contains the Waypoint
Content.

**Chapter Dateline**
Pattern: `x3p0-a-boy-in-the-wild/chapter-dateline`

The default opening of the Chapter Header. Shows the season followed by a
period, then the post excerpt as a note or observation. May show any
combination of available metadata — season, day, chapter number, year, time
of day — depending on what is most relevant to the chapter's opening moment.

**Chapter Header separator**
An `is-style-separator-draw` separator between the dateline group and the
Chapter Content. Present by default. May change style or be removed if the
chapter's opening moment calls for it.

**Story Navigation wrapper**
Pattern: `x3p0-a-boy-in-the-wild/story-navigation-default-full`

Full-width `<footer>` wrapper for the Story Navigation Content, with a
separator above the navigation links.

**Chapter Footer**
Built inline in each chapter pattern — no pattern file. The default contains
the bird horizon icon centred above a flex row of post title, inline
separators, day fragment, and season fragment. Its form should serve the
chapter's ending, not a fixed template.

**Canvas**
No default — each chapter specifies its own canvas effect or omits one
entirely. The canvas element is always the last child inside the Entry Group.
See the canvas-effects skill before writing or modifying any canvas script.

---

### The principle

The pattern structure exists to give every chapter a consistent foundation
and a consistent reader experience — navigation in, navigation out, the
chapter's colour world, the persistent interface. Within that foundation, no
chapter should feel like any other. The defaults are a starting point, not a
ceiling.
